The author's research notes at redam94.github.io/second-brain. Most summarize a primary paper listed further down this page.

Causal inference and identification

  • The Experimental Idealselection-bias decomposition; randomization removes selection; balance checks (STAR example).
  • Synthetic Controlsynthetic control definition and placebo inference.
  • Synthetic Control Requirements Summarizes Abadie (2021)effect size vs volatility, pre-period length, structural breaks, donor pool, no anticipation, spillover as a lower bound, convex hull, fewer than about 5 donors, pre-registration.
  • Synthetic Control Inference and DiagnosticsRMSPE ratio, permutation p-values, pre-fit filtering, one-sided tests, backdating, leave-one-out.
  • Synthetic Control Extensionspenalized, bias-corrected/augmented, elastic-net and matrix-completion variants; conformal and predictive intervals.
  • Generalized Synthetic Control Method Summarizes Xu (2017)interactive fixed effects, cross-validated factors, bootstrap; needs many controls and a long pre-period.
  • Differences-in-Differencesthe parallel-trends assumption.
  • Bayesian Difference in DifferencesDiD as a Bayesian regression in PyMC with counterfactual draws.
  • DiD with Multiple Time Periods: Overview Summarizes Callaway & Sant'Anna (2021)the TWFE critique; minimum-wage example (about -3.1% vs a TWFE null).
  • Identifying Assumptions for Staggered DiDanticipation horizon, never-treated vs not-yet-treated comparisons, no pre-testing.
  • Brodersen 2015: OverviewBSTS components; DiD and synthetic control as special cases of CausalImpact.
  • CausalImpact Empirical Application95 of 190 DMAs, 6 weeks, 22% [13%, 30%] lift; 21% with observational controls; placebo 2%.
  • Counterfactual Impact Estimationpointwise, cumulative and running-average impact; intervals that widen with horizon.

Advertising evidence and experimental design

  • Activity Bias in Advertising Summarizes Lewis, Rao & Reiley (2011)Yahoo! experiments: RCT +5.4% vs observational +872% to +1198%.
  • Observational vs Experimental Methods in Advertisingcontrols do not fix activity bias; "a bias problem, not a variance problem".
  • Logic of Regression Adjustmentcausal logic of covariate adjustment (the basis of CUPED-style variance reduction).
  • Power Analysis and Sample Sizealpha, power, MDE; two-means and two-proportions sample-size formulas; attrition adjustment.
  • Type S and Type M Errorswrong-sign and exaggeration errors in underpowered studies; multilevel models as a remedy.
  • Multiple Testing Correctionsfamily-wise error growth with many tests; Bonferroni, Holm, Benjamini-Hochberg.
  • Partial Pooling as Multiple Comparisons Correctionhierarchical shrinkage as an alternative to classical corrections.
  • Multiple Comparisons: Bayesian PerspectiveBayesian view of multiplicity (further reading).
  • Garden of Forking Pathsdata-contingent analysis inflates false positives; motivates pre-registration.
  • Researcher Degrees of Freedomanalytic flexibility and pre-specification.

Media mix and market response models

  • Bayesian Media Mix Modeling: Overview Summarizes Jin et al. (2017)MMM definition, the combined adstock + Hill equation, why Bayesian.
  • Carryover (Adstock) Functional Formsgeometric and delayed adstock; 13-week maximum lag.
  • Shape (Saturation) EffectsHill and reach transforms; identifiability; no extrapolation beyond observed spend.
  • Bayesian Estimation and Priors for MMMtypical priors, prior dominance, prior sensitivity.
  • ROAS, mROAS, and Optimal Media MixROAS and mROAS definitions, per-draw computation, multimodal optimal mix.
  • MMM Model Selection and Application2-year vs 60-year simulation bias, fit criteria are not causal validity, shampoo case study, residual autocorrelation.
  • Advertising and Promotion Effectsshort-run ad elasticity about 0.10; long-run effect about 2x; 6 to 9 month duration for mature goods.
  • Market Response Models: Overviewthe market response modeling lineage behind MMM.

Geo experiments

Design, estimation and tooling for region-level tests.

  • Measuring Ad Effectiveness Using Geo Experiments Vaver & Koehler (2011), Google Researchrandomized geo design and the rationale for it.
  • Estimating Ad Effectiveness using Geo Experiments in a Time-Based Regression Framework Kerman, Wang & Vaver (2017), Google ResearchTime-Based Regression (TBR) for few geos, down to one test vs one control market.caveat Author list follows the research.google page; one search summary listed only Wang & Vaver.
  • A Time-Based Regression Matched Markets Approach for Designing Geo Experiments Google Researchmatched-markets design.
  • Estimating causal effects using geo experiments Unofficial Google Data Science blog (2016)geo-based regression, 30+ geos, just over 210 US geos, 4 to 8 week pre-period, 3 to 5 week tests, cooldown, iROAS, stratified randomization, dominant markets, identity problems at user level.
  • google/matched_markets GitHubTBR analysis plus greedy matched-markets design search under constraints.
  • google/trimmed_match GitHub; Chen & Au (2019), Chen, Longfils & Remy (2021)paired randomized geo designs and robust iROAS.
  • GeoLift: Walkthrough Metadata schema, market-selection power simulation, augmented synthetic control options, L2 imbalance, purchase-cycle duration.Also: GeoLift homecaveat The conformal-inference default is documented in secondary pages and a third-party replication, not confirmed on Meta's main pages.
  • GeoLift Best Practices Metapre-period 4 to 5x the test length, 25+ periods, 20+ geos, 52 weeks of history, daily data preferred, holding other media constant.
  • Inverse GeoLift Meta GeoLift bloggo-dark holdouts, holdout sizing, opportunity cost, MMM calibration caveat.
  • GeoLift: Incorporating Commuting Zones Metacommuting zones to account for the commuter effect.caveat Using commuting zones to limit spillover is the research brief's inference.
  • Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness Blake, Nosko & Tadelis (2015), EconometricaeBay's 68-DMA, 60-day go-dark test; no measurable short-term benefit from brand-keyword ads; organic substitution.

User-level experiments

Randomized tests on individual identities, the platforms that run them, and the evidence on observational alternatives.

  • A Comparison of Approaches to Advertising Measurement: Evidence from Big Field Experiments at Facebook Gordon, Zettelmeyer, Bhargava & Chapsky (2019), Marketing Science 38(2)15 Facebook conversion-lift RCTs; observational methods often fail to recover the experimental effect.Also: Working paper PDF, RePEc record
  • Close Enough? Gordon, Moakler & Zettelmeyer (2023), Marketing Science 42(4)663 Facebook experiments; double/debiased ML and propensity matching do not reliably recover RCT lift.caveat Figures differ between arXiv versions. The current version reports median RCT lifts of 29%, 18% and 5% (upper, mid, lower funnel), median DML lift estimates of 83%, 58% and 24%, and a median absolute RCT-vs-DML difference of 115, 103 and 57 percentage points.
  • The Unfavorable Economics of Measuring the Returns to Advertising Lewis & Rao (2015), QJE 130(4)25 large experiments (about $2.8M of spend); coefficient of variation about 10; more than 10 million person-weeks; median ROI interval over 100 percentage points wide.Also: EconPapers, IDEAS
  • Here, There, and Everywhere: Correlated Online Behaviors Can Lead to Overestimates of the Effects of Advertising Lewis, Rao & Reiley (2011), WWW 2011activity bias in observational ad measurement.caveat DOI confirmed via search; the publisher page was not reachable during research.
  • Ghost Ads Johnson, Lewis & Nubbemeyer (2017), Journal of Marketing Research 54(6); NBER conference versionghost-ad design; PSA bias in retargeting; similar precision at a fraction of the cost of PSA or ITT tests; 17.2% site-visit and 10.8% sales lift (abstract: 17% and 11%).caveat The journal DOI (10.1509/jmr.15.0297) could not be opened during research, so the conference PDF is linked.
  • Inferno: A Guide to Field Experiments in Online Display Advertising no link Johnson (2023), Journal of Economics & Management Strategy 32(3): 469-490tiny display-ad effects need large experiments; power, compliance, identity fragmentation, spillovers.caveat Not opened directly: bibliographic details come from search results and the SSRN page blocked automated access, so no link is given.
  • Lift Study guide Meta Marketing APIrandomization of Accounts Center accounts into test and control cells; settings locked at start; limited access.
  • About Conversion Lift Google Ads Helpuser-based vs geography-based Conversion Lift.
  • Comparing lift types Google Ads Helpcampaign-type availability; geography-based lift supports offline data and typically needs a higher budget.
  • Ads Data Hub Google developer docsan example of an ad-platform clean room.
  • User Privacy and Data Use Apple DeveloperApp Tracking Transparency permission is required to track users or access the advertising identifier.
  • Privacy Sandbox next steps Privacy Sandbox blog (April 22, 2025)Chrome keeps its current third-party cookie choice; no standalone prompt.
  • CUPED: Improving the Sensitivity of Online Controlled Experiments by Utilizing Pre-Experiment Data Deng, Xu, Kohavi & Walker (2013), WSDMpre-experiment covariates reduce variance; the covariate must be unaffected by treatment.

  • Meridian: Channel calibration recommendation and score Google Meridian docswhich channels to test next.
