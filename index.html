What incrementality testing measures.

An incrementality test asks one question: what would have happened without the ads? It answers by building a counterfactual, a control group that shows the outcome the test group would have had, and reading lift as the difference.

Comparing people who saw an ad with people who did not looks like a measurement, but it mixes the ad's effect with selection bias: ads reach people who were already more likely to convert. Random assignment makes treatment independent of potential outcomes, so the selection term drops out1.

There are two common ways to randomize for media. A geo-level test assigns whole regions to test or control and compares aggregate outcomes per region. A user-level test assigns individual identities (accounts, devices, cookies, hashed emails) and compares outcomes per person.

Methods that do not randomize, such as media mix models and multi-touch attribution, infer the counterfactual from correlations instead. They are causal only when their identifying assumptions hold11.

Why not just compare exposed and unexposed users?

Because the people an ad reaches differ from the people it misses. In Yahoo! experiments, the randomized lift on brand searches was +5.4%, while observational estimates on the same campaign ranged from +872% (with day, session, page-view and minutes controls) to +1198% (no controls)23. Adding controls barely helped: it is a bias problem, not a variance problem.

Two designs

Geo vs user-level, at a glance.

Both are randomized experiments. They differ in what gets randomized, and that one choice decides which channels, outcomes and questions each can handle.

Geo-level testing

Randomize regions, not people.

Change media in some markets, hold it steady in others, and compare an aggregate KPI per market.

Needs no user identity and can capture offline sales and delayed conversions6. The catch is few units: the US has just over 210 geos (DMAs), so intervals are wide6.

Unit: DMA, state, commuting zone Identity: none Reads: total business, offline
Explore geo testing

User-level testing

Randomize people, not places.

Hold out a random share of identities and compare their outcomes with the users who were eligible for ads.

Unbiased by design and fine-grained enough to compare creative, audiences or frequency19. It depends on addressable identity, which privacy changes are eroding8.

Unit: account, device, cookie Identity: required Reads: tracked conversions

Quick decision guide

Which test should I use?

Answer the question that matches your situation. These are starting points; the comparison page covers the trade-offs in detail.

  1. Is your KPI only observed offline or in aggregate, such as retail, call-center or dealer sales?

    Geo test. It needs no user identity and works on first-party sales data6.

  2. Is the channel impossible to target person by person, like linear TV, radio, out-of-home or print?

    Geo test. Change spend by market instead of by user.

  3. Can the platform randomize logged-in identities, and can conversions be joined back to them?

    User-level test, such as a platform conversion-lift study or a ghost-ads design79.

  4. Are you comparing creative, audiences, bidding or frequency inside one platform?

    User-level multi-cell test. Geo tests rarely have the power to separate variants.

  5. Do you need total-business or cross-channel incrementality, including substitution from organic traffic?

    Geo test. eBay's go-dark test in 68 DMAs found brand-keyword search ads had no measurable short-term benefit because traffic moved to organic search10.

  6. Is the channel always on, and do you want to limit the revenue you put at risk?

    Go-dark holdout (Inverse GeoLift). Switch media off only in the treated markets, instead of the more than half of locations a standard GeoLift often needs as controls12.

  7. Did a clear intervention happen that you could not randomize, such as a launch or a market entry?

    Quasi-experiment: CausalImpact or synthetic control. See other methods.

  8. Do you need to allocate budget across every channel, including long-run effects?

    Media mix model, calibrated with the experiments above13.

Beyond experiments

The measurement landscape.

No single method is both causal and comprehensive. Each answers a different question, and they work best when they check one another.

Media mix modeling

Strategic budget allocation across every channel.

A regression of aggregate sales on spend by channel, with carryover and saturation14. Covers offline media and needs no identity, but it is correlational unless calibrated with experiments.

Learn more

Multi-touch attribution

Tactical, in-flight optimization of digital tactics.

Splits credit for each conversion across touchpoints on the path. It measures association, not incrementality, and inherits activity and targeting bias24.

Learn more

CausalImpact

Impact of a launch you could not randomize.

A Bayesian structural time-series model forecasts the counterfactual from unaffected control series. It assumes those controls stay unaffected and stable15.

Planning any test

Most failed tests fail before launch.

An underpowered test reported as "no effect" is the classic mistake. Power, duration and the decision rule should be written down before any data comes in1617.

Read the planning guide

  1. State the decision and estimand

    Name the decision the test feeds and the number it produces: iROAS, lift % or incremental conversions.

  2. Size it for a realistic effect

    Derive the minimum detectable effect from break-even economics, then simulate or calculate power.

  3. Choose units and split

    Pick markets or audiences, keep controls clean of spillover, and balance power against opportunity cost.

  4. Fix duration and cooldown

    Run at least one full purchase cycle and keep measuring after media stops to catch lagged conversions.

  5. Pre-register

    Write down the KPI, estimator, window and decision rule before launch, to avoid the garden of forking paths.

  6. Read out the interval

    Report the confidence or credible interval, not just significance, and feed the result into your MMM.
