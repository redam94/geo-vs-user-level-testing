Beyond the lift test

The methods around the test.

Media mix models, attribution, quasi-experiments and response benchmarks each answer a different question. This page shows what each one can and cannot tell you, and how lift tests tie them together.

The big picture

Observational vs experimental evidence.

Every measurement method builds a counterfactual. The difference that matters is how: by randomizing, or by inferring it from data nobody randomized.

Experiments (user-level RCTs, ghost ads, conversion lift, geo experiments) create the counterfactual by randomization: the control group shows what would have happened without the ads. Observational methods (MMM, MTA, matching, regression on logs) infer it from correlations, and are causal only when their identifying assumptions hold.

In advertising, the evidence that this distinction matters is unusually strong. Users who see an ad are more active online that day anyway, so exposed-vs-unexposed comparisons credit the ad with that extra activity. This is activity bias, and observational estimates land far above the RCT benchmark even with rich controls12. In the study the note summarizes, the RCT showed a 5.4% increase in brand-related searches, while observational estimates ran from 1198% with no controls to 872% with all controls1.

Gordon, Zettelmeyer, Bhargava & Chapsky compared commonly used observational methods with 15 large Facebook RCTs (about 500M user-experiment observations and 1.6B impressions). The methods "often fail to accurately measure the true effect"3.

Experiments are hard too

Across 25 large digital-ad RCTs, individual sales were so noisy relative to per-person ad cost that the median ROI confidence interval was more than 100 percentage points wide. Informative experiments can need more than 10M person-weeks5. Unbiased does not mean precise.

  • 83 / 58 / 24% Median lift estimated by double/debiased ML for upper, mid and lower funnel outcomes, against median RCT lifts of 29 / 18 / 5%, in 663 Facebook experiments with 5,000+ user features Gordon, Moakler & Zettelmeyer (2023)4
  • 173 / 176 / 64% Median lift estimated by stratified propensity-score matching on the same experiments and funnel stages Gordon, Moakler & Zettelmeyer (2023)4
  • >100 pp Median width of the ROI confidence interval across 25 large ad experiments Lewis & Rao (2015)5

Practitioner takeaway

Experiments are the most credible evidence available, but they are expensive, narrow and noisy. Observational models are cheap, broad and continuous, but biased in ways that are hard to see. Modern measurement combines both: experiments calibrate models, and models generalize experiments.

Media mix modeling

Bayesian MMM: carryover and diminishing returns.

An MMM regresses aggregate outcomes on media by channel, plus controls, to answer two questions: how much did each channel contribute, and how should the budget move?

The model uses weekly or monthly sales, national or by geo, against spend or exposure per channel and controls such as price, distribution, promotions, seasonality and macro factors6. The canonical Bayesian specification from Jin, Wang, Sun, Chan & Koehler (Google, 2017) is7:

yt = τ + Σm βm · Hill(adstock(xt−L+1..t, m); Km, Sm) + Σc γc zt,c + εt

Media effects are additive, so channels do not interact, and adstock is applied before saturation6.

Adstock: advertising keeps working after exposure

Carryover comes from delayed response, purchase cycles and word of mouth. The common functional forms8:

  • Geometric adstock weights lag l by αl with retention 0 < α < 1. The effect peaks in the exposure week and decays. It is the marketing analogue of the Koyck distributed lag.
  • Delayed (peak) adstock weights by α(l−θ)², peaking θ periods after exposure. Useful for brand TV. Jin et al. use L = 13 weeks as the maximum lag.
  • Weibull CDF/PDF adstock in Robyn. The PDF form allows a lagged peak and is recommended for longer conversion windows9.

Benchmarks from the market-response literature: the long-run ad effect is roughly double the short-run effect, and about 90% of the effect for mature packaged goods dissipates within 6–9 months10.

Saturation: the shape of diminishing returns

The Hill function 1 / (1 + (x/K)−S) has half-saturation point K and shape S. S > 1 gives an S-curve and S ≤ 1 a concave curve. The reach transform fixes S = 1 and is more parsimonious11.

Identifiability: do not extrapolate

Very different (K, S, β) triples can produce almost the same curve over the observed spend range. The curve is estimable inside the data range. The individual parameters, and any extrapolation beyond observed spend, are not11.

Bayesian estimation

Priors do a lot of the work.

Adstock and Hill transforms make the model nonlinear, and one MMM dataset carries little information relative to its parameter count. Priors fill the gap, for better or worse.

Bayesian MCMC lets prior knowledge fill the information gap12. Typical priors are:

  • beta or uniform for the retention rate α;
  • uniform for the peak delay θ;
  • gamma for the shape S;
  • beta over the observed range for the half-saturation K;
  • half-normal (non-negative) for the media coefficient β.

Chan & Perry (Google, 2017) frame MMM challenges as data limitations, selection bias and modeling, and note that MMM estimates are correlational and causal only under narrow conditions14.

Prior dominance

In simulations with two years of weekly data, saturation curves were underestimated by about 18–33% at a reference spend level. With sixty years of data the curves were close to unbiased. The posterior can look almost like the prior, and there is "no universally correct prior"1213.

More history is not the practical fix, because markets drift. Better options are pooling across brands or geos (hierarchical models) and informative priors from experiments12.

Budget decisions

ROAS tells you the average. mROAS tells you where to move money.

ROASm
Incremental revenue from channel m divided by its spend. Computed by setting the channel's spend to zero over a change period and summing predicted differences through the carryover tail (t1 + L − 1)15.
mROASm
Revenue from one more unit of spend. Jin et al. use a +1% spend perturbation15. A high-ROAS channel may already be saturated, so reallocation should follow mROAS, not ROAS practitioner framing

Pitfall: plugging in posterior means

Compute ROAS and mROAS per posterior draw, then summarize. Plugging posterior-mean parameters into the formulas ignores their correlations and is wrong15.

The optimal mix can be untrustworthy

The posterior of the optimal mix can be multimodal and very wide. In the shampoo case study (2.5 years of weekly data, 5 channels), the TV/magazine optimum was bimodal at the extremes, and the authors concluded the recommended allocation was not trustworthy where it extrapolates1513.

MMM in practice

Data, tools and trade-offs.

MMM needs aggregate data and no identity, which makes it privacy-durable. It needs variation in spend, which always-on budgets often lack.

Typical MMM data requirements
DimensionTypical requirement
GranularityWeekly (sometimes daily). National, or geo-level (DMA or region) for more variation and hierarchical pooling6.
HistoryUsually 2–3 years of weekly data. The Jin et al. case used about 2.5 years (n = 106 weeks). More is not always better because of non-stationarity13.
Media inputsSpend and/or impressions, GRPs or reach by channel. Spend must vary: flat, always-on spend is unidentifiable practitioner heuristic
ControlsPrice, promotion, distribution, seasonality, holidays, competitor activity, macro factors. Highly correlated controls can need orthogonalization: in the case study, STAN runtime fell from 3,040 s to 75 s13.
KPISales, revenue, conversions or volume, defined consistently over time.
IdentityNone. Aggregate data needs no cookies or device IDs.

MMM strengths

  • Covers all channels, including offline (TV, OOH, print).
  • Privacy-durable: aggregate data, no identity.
  • Captures carryover and long-run effects.
  • Supports budget planning and scenario analysis.
  • Bayesian versions give uncertainty bands.

MMM weaknesses

  • Correlational. Model choice by BIC/WAIC picks the best regression, not the best causal model13.
  • Sensitive to priors and specification12.
  • Slow: quarterly or annual refresh.
  • Low granularity: no creative- or audience-level detail.
  • Cannot extrapolate beyond observed spend11.
  • Selection bias, for example paid search spend that rises because demand rises.

The key link

Calibrating MMM with lift tests.

MMM priors dominate in small samples. A well-run lift test supplies unbiased, local information about one channel's effect, often exactly what the model lacks. The main tools use it in three different ways.

  1. As a prior (Meridian)

    The experiment's ROI estimate and standard error become an ROI prior, for example lognormal. CalibrationBuilder adjusts for spend, recency and duration, combines several experiments by Bayesian updating and fits a parametric prior1819.

  2. As extra likelihood (PyMC-Marketing)

    Each test is a row: channel, baseline spend x, spend change Δx, measured lift Δy, standard error σ. The model's implied f(x + Δx) − f(x) must match Δy within σ, so tests act as "additional observations of the saturation curve"17.

  3. As a selection objective (Robyn)

    MAPE.LIFT penalizes candidate models whose decomposed channel contribution over the test window differs from the experiment. Experiments are treated as ground truth9.

Pitfall: estimand mismatch

MMM ROI uses a zero-spend counterfactual. Many experiments measure marginal lift (a +X% spend increase) or lift against business-as-usual. Translating between them adds uncertainty beyond the experiment's standard error. Check each experiment's recency, duration, granularity, population and complexity before using it18.

A user-level conversion-lift test on one platform measures that platform's on-platform audience and attribution window, not total-business offline sales. Scale and convert carefully practitioner heuristic

Choose experiments with the MMM in mind

Prioritize channels with large spend, wide MMM posteriors or implausible ROI. Meridian's channel-calibration score flags such channels20. Run tests at the spend levels the MMM will extrapolate to, and refresh them over time because effects drift practitioner heuristic. See Planning for test design.

Dissent

Some practitioners argue that injecting experiment results into an MMM can distort its other parameters. Treat calibration as Bayesian evidence with honest uncertainty, not as a hard constraint. vendor opinion, not peer-reviewed

Multi-touch attribution

Attribution measures association, not incrementality.

MTA assigns credit for each conversion across the user-level touchpoints on the path to purchase. It is fast and granular, and correlational by construction.

Rule-based versions include last-click, first-touch, linear, time-decay and position-based. Algorithmic or data-driven versions use Shapley values or Markov chains. Its data needs are user- or device-level event logs (impressions, clicks, conversions), a stable identity graph, and consistent timestamps and conversion windows.

MTA credits touches on converting paths but never compares against people who were not exposed. It therefore inherits activity bias and targeting selection bias: the findings of Lewis, Rao & Reiley and Gordon et al. apply directly to user-level observational credit assignment23.

Good for

  • Fast, granular, in-flight optimization of digital tactics: keywords, creatives, placements, audiences practitioner heuristic
  • Small, frequent decisions within a channel.
  • Real-time or daily feedback.

Limits

  • Correlational: measures association, not incrementality3.
  • Over-credits lower-funnel and retargeting, which target people already likely to convert, and under-credits upper-funnel, offline and view-through media practitioner heuristic
  • Third-party cookie restrictions, mobile tracking opt-in rules and walled gardens break cross-channel paths policy details not verified
  • Cannot see offline channels (TV, OOH, radio) or long-run brand effects.
  • Platform-reported attribution comes from the seller of the media: a directional signal only practitioner heuristic

Incrementality multipliers

Experiments can produce incrementality multipliers (experiment iROAS ÷ attributed ROAS) that rescale MTA credit by channel. This is a common practice, not a standardized method. practitioner heuristic

Quasi-experiments

CausalImpact and synthetic control.

When randomization is not possible but a clear intervention exists (a launch, a market entry, a channel switched off, a non-random geo test), forecast what would have happened and compare.

CausalImpact (BSTS)

Brodersen et al. (2015) fit a Bayesian structural time-series model on the pre-period, with local level/trend, seasonality and a regression on control series using spike-and-slab covariate selection. The model forecasts the post-period counterfactual. Impact is observed minus counterfactual, reported pointwise, cumulatively and as a running average with credible intervals2122.

It generalizes difference-in-differences and synthetic control by adding trend, seasonality and time-varying uncertainty21.

Synthetic control

Synthetic control builds the counterfactual for one treated unit as a weighted combination of donor units matched on the pre-period, with inference from permutation or placebo tests26. It underpins geo-test tools such as GeoLift; see Geo testing.

Assumptions (official docs)

The control series are not affected by the intervention, and the pre-period relationship between controls and target stays stable in the post-period24. Neither can be fully verified from the data. Spillover into controls or a concurrent shock breaks the estimate.

Evidence from an ad campaign

A 6-week search campaign geo-randomized to 95 of 190 DMAs, analyzed three ways23.

  • 22% Lift with randomized controls, interval [13%, 30%] CausalImpact empirical application23
  • 21% Lift with observational controls (industry search volume), interval [12%, 30%] CausalImpact empirical application23
  • 2% Placebo test on untreated DMAs, interval [−6%, 10%] CausalImpact empirical application23

Planning a CausalImpact analysis

  • Long, clean pre-periodThe paper's ad example used about 1 year of baseline data23.
  • Predictive but unexposed controlsContaminated controls bias the counterfactual24.
  • Keep the post-period short enoughIntervals widen with horizon, so long post-periods lose power25.
  • Pick the right summaryCumulative impact for flow KPIs (sales), running average for stock KPIs (subscribers)25.
  • Always run placebo or backtest checksFake intervention dates and untreated units.
  • Know its rankMuch stronger than naive before/after or MTA, weaker than randomization.

Market response models

Benchmarks for a sanity check.

MMM descends from the econometric market response tradition: the 4Ps, Koyck distributed lags, transfer functions, cointegration and market-share models. Its empirical generalizations make useful priors and plausibility checks.

  • ≈0.10 Short-run advertising elasticity (meta-analytic range 0.10–0.22); 0.10 is a recommended conservative null Advertising and Promotion Effects10
  • ≈2× Long-run effect relative to the short-run effect Advertising and Promotion Effects10
  • 0.26 vs 0.05 Elasticity of new vs established products (Lodish et al. 1995): elasticities decline as brands mature Advertising and Promotion Effects10

Price elasticity is larger in magnitude than advertising elasticity10. For the wider lineage, see the market response models overview27.

How to use them

If an MMM or MTA implies an advertising elasticity far above these benchmarks for a mature brand, check it for bias before acting on it.

Unified measurement

One loop, four kinds of evidence.

No single method is both causal and comprehensive. Give each method the question it answers best and let them check one another. practitioner framework

MMM

Strategic layer.

Cross-channel budget allocation, offline and long-run effects, scenario planning. Refreshed quarterly.

Experiments

Causal ground truth.

Geo and user-level tests validate and calibrate the MMM's channel ROIs and curves, and test big bets and new channels.

MTA

Tactical layer.

In-flight optimization within channels, rescaled by experiment-derived incrementality factors.

Quasi-experiments

Gap fillers.

CausalImpact and synthetic control where randomization is not possible.

The operating loop

  1. MMM flags channels

    Uncertain, high-spend or implausible-ROI channels become test candidates.

  2. Design a geo or user-level test

    At the spend levels the MMM will extrapolate to. See Planning.

  3. Feed the lift into the MMM

    As a prior or as an extra likelihood, with its estimand translated honestly.

  4. Re-optimize the budget

    On mROAS, computed per posterior draw, inside the observed spend range.

  5. Rescale MTA, then repeat

    Apply incrementality factors to attribution, and re-test as effects drift.

When methods disagree

Trust the well-powered experiment for that channel, period and spend level. Then ask why the model differs: selection bias, mis-specified adstock, saturation extrapolation, or estimand mismatch. practitioner heuristic

Validation

Sensitivity analysis checklist.

Observational estimates are only as good as their assumptions. Run these checks before a model's numbers move a budget.

  • Prior sensitivityRefit the MMM under alternative priors (half-normal vs uniform on β, alternative K priors) and report how ROAS and mROAS move. Jin et al. show results can shift substantially12.
  • Specification sensitivityCross adstock {geometric, delayed/Weibull} with saturation {Hill, reach}. Compare by BIC/WAIC and holdout error, remembering that fit criteria are not causal validity13.
  • Residual diagnosticsCheck autocorrelation. Persistent residual autocorrelation out to about 15 weeks signalled misspecification in the case study13.
  • Out-of-sample, time-series CVRobyn uses validation NRMSE as its objective when time-series validation is on9.
  • Parameter recovery on simulated dataFit the model to data generated with known truth to learn what it can identify13.
  • Placebo tests for quasi-experimentsFake intervention dates and untreated units23.
  • Unobserved-confounding sensitivityHow strong would a hidden confounder have to be to explain the effect? no specific method verified
  • Experiment holdback checkCompare the MMM's predicted lift for the test window with the observed lift before calibrating9.

Pitfalls to avoid

  • MMM or MTA output read as causalwithout any experimental anchor.
  • Optimizing on ROAS instead of mROASmoves money into saturated channels.
  • Extrapolating saturation curvesbeyond observed spend11.
  • Posterior means in place of per-draw metricsignores parameter correlations15.
  • Estimand mismatch in calibrationmarginal vs zero-spend, platform vs total business, short window vs carryover18.
  • Paid search selection biassearch spend follows demand.
  • CausalImpact with contaminated controlsor a structural break in the post-period24.
  • Platform attribution read as incrementalityit comes from the seller of the media.
