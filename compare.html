Head to head

Same goal. Different unit.

Geo-level and user-level tests are both randomized experiments. What they randomize decides which channels they can measure, how much data they need, how precise they are, and who gets to run them. This page lines them up dimension by dimension.

Both: randomizedGeo: regions, no identityUser: people, identity required

How to read the table

The cost, causal-strength and "who runs it" entries are a qualitative synthesis from the research notes, not benchmarks; "platform-run studies are often free" is likewise a general observation, not a quoted price. The 2 to 6 week user-level duration is a practitioner convention. The border-spillover mechanisms (TV signals crossing DMA lines, noisy IP geolocation) are practitioner knowledge, not drawn from a cited study.

Fit

When each design earns its place.

Start from the channel and the KPI, not from the tool you already have. These are the conditions where each design is the natural choice, and the signs that it is the wrong one.

When to use geo

The outcome or the channel lives offline.

Reach for a geo test when identity is missing, the media can't be targeted to people, or you need the whole business.

  • KPI observed only in aggregate or offlineRetail, call-center or dealer sales.
  • Channel can't be randomized per personLinear TV, radio, OOH, print, or a walled garden without a lift tool.
  • Total-business or cross-channel effectIncluding halo and organic substitution2.
  • Leaky identityCookie and device loss would contaminate user holdouts1.
  • MMM calibrationExperimental iROAS on the same aggregate KPI the model uses.

Switch designs when the expected effect is small relative to geo-level noise and the test is short13; too few comparable, uncontaminated geos exist (permutation inference is not meaningful with fewer than about 5 donors)13; the treated market looks like no combination of others, as with markets like London or Paris that dominate a country1; or clean user-level randomization is available, which is usually far more powerful per dollar because there are many more units.

Geo testing in depth

When to use user-level

The platform can see and split people.

Reach for a user-level test when exposure and outcomes both attach to a stable identity, and the question is inside one platform.

  • Exposure controlled per identitySocial, programmatic with ghost ads, logged-in CTV, email, CRM, push.
  • Outcomes join to the same IDPixel, CAPI, SDK, or hashed CRM and offline matches.
  • Creative, audience, bidding or frequencyMulti-cell designs separate variants that geo tests rarely can.
  • Large audiences, results in weeksMillions of reachable users.
  • Lower-funnel, high-frequency conversionsWhere the effect-to-noise ratio is most favorable.

Switch designs when identity coverage is poor (heavy iOS opt-out, logged-out audiences), audiences or conversion volumes are small, the question is cross-channel, or you need a read that does not come from the seller of the media.

User-level testing in depth

Trade-offs

Strengths and weaknesses.

Each design's weakness is roughly the other's strength. That is why mature measurement programs run both.

Geo-level testing

Strengths

  • Privacy-durable and identity-free. Works on first-party sales data1.
  • Captures offline sales, delayed conversions and cross-channel effects1.
  • Works for media that can't be targeted to individuals.
  • True randomization is possible with enough geos20, and even matched-market synthetic-control designs can be pre-registered13.
  • Results come as iROAS and incremental units that finance and MMM teams can use.

Weaknesses

  • Few units, so low power: just over 210 US geos and far fewer regions in most countries1, which means wide intervals.
  • Opportunity cost. Go-dark tests forgo revenue, and standard GeoLifts often need more than half of locations as controls10.
  • Spillover across borders, and noisy IP-based geo-targeting in digital platforms practitioner heuristic
  • Local shocks such as weather, competitor promotions or store openings can swamp the signal13.
  • Average effects for the treated geos. Carrying them to other markets or spend levels is an assumption.

User-level testing

Strengths

  • Unbiased by design when randomization is respected1921.
  • Fine-grained: creative, audience, frequency and bidding comparisons through multi-cell designs14.
  • Fast to set up on major platforms, with no long geo histories.
  • CUPED and ghost ads can sharply improve precision per dollar128.
  • Results can be segmented by audience attributes17.

Weaknesses

  • Requires addressable identity, which ATT and browser cookie blocking are eroding34.
  • Noisy: small true effects need huge samples, and wide ROI intervals are the norm7.
  • ITT dilution and cross-device contamination bias lift toward zero.
  • Walled gardens measure themselves, with limited outcome coverage and opaque methods.
  • Can't measure offline-only channels or total cross-channel effects, and spillovers violate SUTVA.

Inputs

What each test needs from your data.

A geo test needs history. A user test needs identity. Check which one you actually have before you design anything.

Geo tests identify the effect by comparing regions over time, so they depend on a long, clean pre-period and complete location-by-date data. User tests identify the effect through randomization alone, so history is optional, but every unit must be traceable from ad delivery to conversion.

Decision flow

Seven questions, in order.

Work down the list and stop at the first answer that settles it. Each step only applies if the earlier ones did not.

  1. 1. Is the KPI only observed offline or in aggregate, with no way to join it to an identity?

    Yes: geo test. It needs no identity and runs on first-party sales data1. No: go to 2.

  2. 2. Can the channel be delivered and withheld person by person?

    No: geo test. Linear TV, radio, OOH and print change by market, not by user. Yes: go to 3.

  3. 3. Do you need total-business or cross-channel incrementality, including substitution?

    Yes: geo test. eBay's 68-DMA, 60-day go-dark showed brand search ads had no measurable short-term benefit because traffic moved to organic search2. No: go to 4.

  4. 4. Does the platform randomize stable, logged-in identities, with conversions joined back and good coverage?

    Yes: user-level test, such as platform conversion lift or ghost ads1412. No (heavy opt-out, cookie-only IDs): prefer a geo test. Cross-device leakage biases cookie-level lift toward zero, so treat it as a lower bound, and remember that opted-out users leave a non-random slice that may not generalize.

  5. 5. Is the question inside one platform: creative, audience, bidding or frequency?

    Yes: user-level multi-cell test. Geo tests rarely have the power to separate variants. No: either design can work; compare power and cost.

  6. 6. Can the design detect the effect you care about?

    Simulate before you commit. Geo: market-selection power simulations on history9. User: N per group = 2(z1−α/2 + z1−β)² σ² / d², so at α = 0.05 and 80% power, (1.96 + 0.84)² ≈ 7.84 and N ≈ 15.7 σ² / d²23. Set d from break-even economics (the lift that makes iROAS = 1), then power the diluted ITT effect: expected TOT lift × expected exposure rate. If neither design is powered, change the KPI, spend contrast or duration.

  7. 7. Is randomization impossible, for example one market or an intervention that already happened?

    Quasi-experiment: TBR, synthetic control or CausalImpact, with placebo checks624. Treat it as weaker than a randomized test.

Chose geo? Pick the design
  • 30 or more geos and you can randomizeStratified randomized geo experiment with GBR, or a paired design with Trimmed Match116.
  • Few geos or one marketTBR or synthetic control, with algorithmic matched-market design615.
  • Ongoing channel, minimal revenue riskInverse GeoLift or a go-dark holdout10.
  • Phased rolloutCallaway–Sant'Anna staggered DiD, not plain two-way fixed effects25.

Chose user-level? Pick the design
  • Intent-to-treat holdoutSimple and randomization-protected, but diluted when few test users see the ad.
  • Ghost adsMeasures the would-be-exposed with no placebo cost, if the platform supports it12.
  • Platform conversion liftMeta or Google randomize accounts into test and holdout cells1417.
  • Owned-channel holdoutEmail, CRM, push or app: you randomize your own customer file.
  • Avoid PSA designs for optimized deliveryThe algorithm serves placebo and brand ads to different people; for retargeting this is "severely biased"12.

About the quasi-experiment step

In the CausalImpact paper's geo example, observational controls gave 21% [12%, 30%] lift against 22% [13%, 30%] with randomized controls24. That agreement is one case study. It does not guarantee observational controls will work in general.

Better together

Hybrid designs, and how both feed MMM.

Experiments are credible but narrow. Models are broad but biased in ways that are hard to see. The working answer is to let experiments calibrate models, and models generalize experiments. Small MMM datasets lean heavily on priors, and well-run lift tests supply the informative ones26.

Ways to combine the two designs

One platform, both modes

Google Conversion Lift comes in a user-based type and a geography-based type. The geo type supports offline data and reports incremental ROAS, conversions and value, but typically needs a higher budget1711.

Geo tests audit platform lift

Platform studies are real RCTs, but the platform grades its own homework. Send your own first-party conversions, replicate in a clean room, and calibrate platform lift against geo tests periodically18.

Rescale attribution

Divide an experiment's iROAS by the attributed ROAS to get an incrementality multiplier, then rescale MTA credit by channel. common practice, not a standard method

Three ways a lift result enters an MMM

  1. As a prior

    Google Meridian turns an experiment's ROI estimate and standard error into an ROI prior. Its CalibrationBuilder adjusts for spend, recency and duration and combines several experiments2728.

  2. As extra data

    PyMC-Marketing adds each lift test as a likelihood term: the model's implied saturation-curve difference must match the measured lift within its standard error29.

  3. As a selection objective

    Meta Robyn's MAPE.LIFT penalizes candidate models whose channel contribution over the test window differs from the experiment30.

Which design feeds the model better?

A geo test reads the same kind of aggregate, total-business KPI an MMM models, including offline sales. That makes its iROAS the more direct calibration input for most models our synthesis, not a cited finding. Note that calibration is more complex for inverse, go-dark designs10.

A user-level conversion-lift test measures one platform's on-platform audience over its attribution window. It does not measure total-business offline sales, so scale and convert it carefully before using it as a prior practitioner guidance

Pick experiments with the model in mind

Prioritize channels with large spend, wide MMM posteriors or implausible ROI. Meridian's channel-calibration score flags them31. Run tests at the spend levels the model will extrapolate to, and refresh them as effects drift practitioner guidance

Estimand mismatch

MMM ROI uses a zero-spend counterfactual. Many experiments measure marginal lift, such as a spend increase, or lift against business as usual. Translating between them adds uncertainty beyond the experiment's standard error. Check each test's recency, duration, granularity, population and complexity first27.

Dissent

Some practitioners argue that forcing experiment results into an MMM can distort other parameters. Treat calibration as Bayesian evidence with honest uncertainty, not a hard constraint. vendor opinion, not peer-reviewed

The operating loop

  1. The MMM flags a channel

    High spend, a wide posterior or an implausible ROI.

  2. Design the right test

    Geo for offline, broadcast or total-business questions; user-level for addressable, in-platform questions. Use the decision flow above.

  3. Feed the lift back

    As a prior, a likelihood term or a selection objective, with the estimand translated honestly.

  4. Re-optimize and rescale

    Reallocate budget on marginal ROAS, rescale attribution, then repeat as effects drift. See MMM and attribution.
